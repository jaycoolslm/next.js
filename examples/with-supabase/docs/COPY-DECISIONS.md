# Copy decisions (282)

Rationale for the wording in `content/**`. Authored by the copy/legal-adjacent
workstream. Everything here is drafting for a record-keeping tool; none of it is
legal advice, and the copy is written so the product never claims otherwise.

## Structure and i18n

- All user-facing strings live in `content/**` as typed constants. Nothing
  UI-facing is hardcoded elsewhere.
- One locale object per file, keyed `"en-GB"` (`Record<"en-GB", ...>`), so a
  second locale is a drop-in later. v1 ships English only (spec §12 defers an
  Arabic UI; the Arabic that does ship is scripture and contract-cover text,
  not UI chrome).
- en-GB spelling throughout ("recognise", "organisation", "authorisation").
- Verbatim strings are hoisted to standalone `const`s
  (`STANDARD_DISCLAIMER`, `CONTRACT_PACK_FOOTER`, `TRIPWIRE_CORE_WARNING`) and
  composed elsewhere, so they cannot drift and a test can assert reuse.

## Verbatim, non-negotiable strings

Reproduced exactly as the spec requires:

- Standard disclaimer (§2.4): "This is an automated information notice, not
  legal advice. Consider consulting a solicitor." — `disclaimers.ts`, and
  appended to every routing note in `routing.ts`.
- Contract-pack footer (§8): "Generated by 282. This document is a template and
  record. It is not legal advice. Take it to a solicitor before relying on it."
  — `disclaimers.ts`.
- Tripwire core warning (§6): "Repeatedly providing finance may amount to
  lending by way of business, which is a criminal offence without FCA
  authorisation. This is not legal advice." — `tripwire.ts`, reused by both the
  amber and red levels.
- The two quoted routing notes (§6, ltd company and first-time
  individual/sole-trader) appear verbatim in `routing.ts`, each followed by the
  standard disclaimer.

## Deliberately avoided

- **No legal-advice claims.** Copy consistently frames output as "automated
  information notices" and "records/templates", and points users to a
  solicitor. The landing page and contract banner say plainly it is not advice.
- **No penalty / interest / late-charge concepts** anywhere, except the
  explicit EXCLUSION clauses (murabaha §3 "No late-payment charges of any
  kind"; qard §2 "Principal only"). Those clauses name the excluded concepts
  only to forbid them — required to make the prohibition legible. Status copy
  for `defaulted` and the dispute/arbitration copy state expressly that no
  charge is added.
- **No marketing of deals / no opportunities surface.** The landing page states
  explicitly, as its first "what it is not" point, that 282 lists no investment
  opportunities, and repeats the firewall framing (no feed, directory, search,
  matching, or recommendation) from spec §2.1 and §12.
- **No fiqh rulings.** Copy describes structure (ownership, risk, ibra',
  principal-only) without issuing a shariah verdict. The receivables/zakat
  wording deferred to the app per §9 (no fiqh ruling given).
- **Ibra' framed as discretionary only.** The early-settlement rebate is
  written as a voluntary act at the financier's sole discretion, never a right
  or promised term, to avoid it reading as a disguised discount-for-early-pay
  entitlement.

## Shariah / structural wording choices

- Murabaha is presented as three separate sections in `contracts.ts`
  (non-binding promise → financier purchase & ownership → separate sale), so the
  document mirrors the shariah-critical sequence the evidence pack must prove
  (§2.2, §5). The promise text stresses non-binding on *both* sides (§5.1). The
  ownership section stresses the financier bears asset risk (§5.3).
- The sale recitals state expressly that the sale is a separate contract made
  only after purchase and acceptance, reinforcing the two-contract structure.
- Qard hasan copy states the principal is the most that can ever be owed and
  that any borrower gift is voluntary and outside the agreement.

## Translation attribution

- Quran 2:282 English rendering uses the **Saheeh International** translation,
  attributed inline wherever shown (`witness.ts`,
  `ayah.translationAttribution`). Only the pertinent excerpt about writing down
  and bringing two witnesses is quoted, not the full (long) verse.
- The Arabic excerpt in `witness.ts` (used on the contract-pack cover per §8)
  is the corresponding opening command and the two-witnesses clause, elided with
  "…"; it is scripture, presented as-is.

## Flags for solicitor review

The following are drafted to template grade but must be reviewed by a qualified
solicitor before any real use. Each contract template in `contracts.ts` carries
the solicitor-review banner (`disclaimers.ts`) at its head.

1. **All contract templates** (`contracts.ts`) — murabaha promise, purchase
   declaration, sale contract, and qard hasan agreement. Generic, not
   circumstance-specific.
2. **Arbitration clause** — references the Arbitration Act 1996, seat England
   and Wales, sole arbitrator; the fallback nomination process is a placeholder
   (`{{arbitrator_nomination_process}}`) the organisation must define and a
   solicitor should confirm is workable.
3. **Regulatory routing notes** (`routing.ts`) — the FCA/consumer-credit
   framing is informational and should be validated against current regulation;
   the "needs review" outcomes deliberately defer to a human.
4. **Tripwire warnings** (`tripwire.ts`) — the "criminal offence" framing is
   verbatim from the spec; confirm it remains accurate wording.
5. **Placeholder tokens** — confirm all `{{snake_case}}` tokens are populated
   and formatted (GBP amounts, en-GB dates) before any pack is relied upon.
